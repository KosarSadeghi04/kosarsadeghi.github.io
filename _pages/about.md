I grew up in Iran and studied economics at the University of Tehran, where I developed a strong interest in understanding how people make decisions and how those decisions are shaped by incentives, institutions, and the environments in which they live.

Economics first drew me in because it offered a rigorous way to study questions about human behavior. Over time, my interests expanded beyond markets and individual choices toward broader questions about how institutions and public policies influence economic and social outcomes.

I am currently pursuing an MA in Global Studies at the University of British Columbia Okanagan. I chose an interdisciplinary program because many of the questions I find most interesting do not fit neatly within a single field. My studies bring together economics, political science, and philosophy, allowing me to approach questions about decision making, institutions, and public policy from different perspectives.

I still approach many of these questions with the analytical perspective of an economist, particularly through applied microeconomics and behavioral economics. At the same time, my interdisciplinary training has made me increasingly interested in the institutional and social contexts in which economic decisions take place.

My current research interests include applied microeconomics, behavioral economics, and public policy. I am particularly interested in individual decision making, incentives, and the interaction between individual behavior and institutions.

Alongside my graduate studies, I have worked on research and data projects involving behavioral interventions, survey data, and large-scale public datasets. I enjoy working with data and building practical tools for collecting, organizing, and analyzing information.

Outside of research, I enjoy reading widely and exploring questions across disciplines. I am interested in ideas that sit between fields and in learning how different approaches can illuminate the same problem from different angles.
