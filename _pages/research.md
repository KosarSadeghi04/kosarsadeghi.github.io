My research interests lie at the intersection of applied microeconomics, behavioral economics, and public policy. I am particularly interested in how individuals make decisions, how incentives and institutions shape those decisions, and how individual behavior translates into broader economic and social outcomes.

I am currently pursuing an MA in Global Studies at the University of British Columbia Okanagan, where my work brings together perspectives from economics, political science, and philosophy. I continue to approach many of my research questions from an economist's perspective, while increasingly interested in the institutional and social contexts in which economic decisions take place.

### Current Research

My current work with Dr. Min Hu spans several areas and methods.

One project examines faculty earnings gaps across Canadian universities. The project uses empirical data to investigate differences in faculty compensation across disciplines and institutions, with a focus on understanding how much of the observed variation can be explained by measurable characteristics and how much remains unexplained. This work has strengthened my interest in applied empirical research and in using data to examine questions about inequality, institutions, and labor markets.

I am also working with Dr. Hu's group on characterizing laminar flame behavior in sustainable aviation fuel surrogates. Although this project lies outside economics, it has given me experience working with experimental and computational data and has reinforced my interest in careful measurement, empirical evidence, and reproducible research.

### Previous Research

My earlier research has focused more directly on individual decision making and behavioral economics.

As part of the BIAS team, I worked on research examining whether behavioral interventions and nudges can influence payment behavior. This experience strengthened my interest in the gap between theoretical models of decision making and how people actually behave in real-world settings.

I have also worked with survey and behavioral data, including the World Values Survey Wave 7 for Iran. Working with large survey datasets has allowed me to explore questions about individual attitudes, preferences, and behavior while developing practical skills in data cleaning, analysis, and empirical research.

Alongside these projects, I have worked on data-focused research through both academic and professional roles. My work has included building data workflows, analyzing datasets, and developing automated tools for collecting and organizing information from multiple public sources. I particularly enjoy the part of research where a conceptual question has to be translated into measurable variables and an empirical strategy.

### Research Interests

Across these projects, I keep returning to a common set of questions:

How do people actually make decisions, as opposed to how economic models assume they do?

How do incentives, institutions, and social environments shape individual behavior?

When do individual choices aggregate into broader economic and social outcomes?

And how should empirical evidence inform the design and evaluation of public policy?

I am especially interested in applied microeconomics, behavioral economics, public policy, economic decision making, incentives, and institutions. My goal is to develop research that combines rigorous empirical methods with a broader understanding of the social and institutional contexts in which economic behavior occurs.

This page will evolve as my research develops. The questions I am working on today are part of a longer process of figuring out where my interests converge and what kind of economist and interdisciplinary researcher I want to become.
